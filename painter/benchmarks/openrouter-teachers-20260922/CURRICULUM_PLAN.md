# Quality-first painting curriculum — MiMo Flash and MiMo Pro

**Decision, 2026-09-24.** Use `xiaomi/mimo-v2.6-flash` for broad candidate generation and `xiaomi/mimo-v2.6-pro` for difficult subjects and revision of promising but visually weak Flash paintings. The 40-reference benchmark is a teacher comparison, not the training distribution: its crowded COCO photos are too difficult to dominate the first teaching stage. No speed or stroke budget is applied until a student paints well without one. This document is a data-generation and training plan; it does not claim that new teacher examples have been generated or admitted.

**Hypothesis.** A 27B student trained first on attractive, faithful paintings of simple references, then on increasingly difficult subjects and real canvas-feedback revisions, will beat the frozen photo-curriculum SFT step-512 initializer on held-out visual preference and reference fidelity. The existing RL step-24 adapter is diagnostic, not an established visual winner. The previous SFT had only 86 admitted real-photo demonstrations alongside 2,400 procedural references; more of the same procedural shape data alone is unlikely to close the visual gap.

## Sources and progression

The unit of coverage is a distinct **scene/reference**, not a prompt paraphrase or a sampled row. Keep source IDs and exact image hashes. Reserve held-out scenes before teacher generation; do not move benchmark or development references into training. COCO object-count labels are only a shortlist signal: manual inspection of low-count COCO examples still found cluttered backgrounds, text, awkward crops, and complex anatomy. A simple tier therefore requires visual review of the actual reference, not a `<=2 boxes` rule.

| Stage | Target accepted scenes in first 600 | References | Main lesson |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| A: clean form | 120 | A single flower, fruit, cup, vase, toy, house, or boat; simple but attractive photo or painted reference; clear silhouette and restrained background | Composition, coherent palette, brush marks, clean complete first painting |
| B: recognizable subject | 240 | One animal, vehicle, plant, food object, or small building in a natural but readable setting | Identity, pose, perspective, lighting, subject/background separation |
| C: relations | 180 | Two or three subjects with one clear spatial relation: cup on table, two flowers in vase, bus at curb, dog beside chair | Count, overlap, scale, grounding, intentional detail hierarchy |
| D: complex transfer | 60 | Busier interiors, articulated people/animals, varied light and real-photo clutter | Preserve salient structure while simplifying nonessential detail |

This is an initial **accepted-scene target**, not a generation quota or a claimed sample-complexity threshold. Start with a 120-scene accepted tranche spanning A and B, inspect the student's held-out paintings, then build toward 600 with extra weight in families that still fail. Do not postpone all hard subjects until the end: every training phase retains a small sample from the preceding and following stages. Keep at least 100 distinct held-out scenes across the same subject families, including difficult real photos; no training variant of a held-out scene is allowed.

The initial reference pool may supply a few visually approved simple cases, but the 100 COCO128 training photos cannot supply this entire curriculum. Add varied, provenance-recorded references rather than copying, cropping, or recoloring those hundred into artificial scene counts. A stylized simple reference can teach brush control; real photos must remain substantial throughout so success is not confined to synthetic shapes. Track reference source and tier separately in results.

## Teacher demonstration contract

Both teachers receive the same reference and painting contract. They make a complete valid first painting, inspect the **actual rendered canvas** alongside the reference, identify the largest visible mismatch or aesthetic defect, and revise only when the canvas can improve. The standard episode ceiling is six turns; allow up to twelve for selected hard examples or a promising painting needing substantial refinement. These are ceilings, not forced turn counts. An early finish is acceptable after a clearly complete painting; a turn-limit canvas is not automatically a finished demonstration. Preserve each program, actual canvas, tool feedback, correction, timing, token usage, model ID, and reference hash. Provider reasoning is evidence, not a default SFT target.

Run Flash first on broad A/B coverage. Use Pro for a deliberate minority of B/C/D cases and for Flash candidates whose composition is promising but whose form, color, or finish needs correction. Preserve teacher identity and cost per **accepted** example. Do not spend Pro calls fixing a fundamentally wrong or incoherent reference interpretation; regenerate or replace that reference. Avoid repeated unbounded calls on one image.

Admission requires a valid rendered painting and a visual decision about both **faithfulness and beauty**. Reject dislocated or extra anatomy, malformed faces, implausible wheel/wing/limb attachments, accidental black voids, muddy overpainting, glaring mechanical repetition, broken perspective that changes subject identity, and unfinished flat blocks. A program that executes is only a candidate. For each admitted episode record why the final canvas is appealing, what it gets right about the reference, and its remaining weakness. Compare selected Flash/Pro candidates blind where both are valid; do not combine validity and beauty into a single additive score. Allow a clearly better intermediate canvas to become a *separately labeled* teacher target only when its exact observed state and turn are retained; never relabel it as the final model output.

## Text-prompt-to-paint slice

Yes, include text-only painting requests, because an artist copilot should handle both a supplied image and a verbal brief. Start with **10% of supervised assistant tokens** from text-only prompts, not 10% of rows. A text prompt specifies visible content, count, spatial relation, palette/light or mood, and optional painterly direction; it does not smuggle an unseen image into the message. Use the same paint/inspect/revise action contract and screen the rendered output for beauty and prompt adherence. Put text-only tasks under an explicit task mode in the data record. The training message itself should clearly identify whether a reference image exists.

For the first mixed SFT, target token exposure of roughly **55% image-reference complete paintings, 20% image-reference visual corrections, 10% text-prompt paintings, and 15% previously validated contract/general retention**. These are starting mixture settings, not established optima. Mix modes within training rather than training text-only last. Do not pair many paraphrases with the same target program or let text-only rows replace image-conditioned coverage. Group all variants of one underlying scene in one split. Evaluate image-reference painting, text-only painting, and image-plus-style-instruction separately; decrease the text share if reference following regresses.

Example text brief: “Paint a single terracotta pot with three white daisies on a pale blue windowsill. Warm morning light enters from the left; keep the flowers distinct and the background quiet.” This is a prompt schema example, **not an accepted training target**.

## Training and one useful decision point

Train from the public, frozen SFT step-512 adapter. Preserve complete teacher trajectories and separate first-paint and visual-correction targets; do not supervise hidden reasoning or a correction against a canvas the student was never shown. Compare checkpoints at the same inference turns and token ceiling against step 512 and unadapted 27B on the held-out scenes. Report valid final canvases, explicit finishes, blind pairwise preference with ties/uncertainty, subject-family failures, and tokens/turn. Keep a small human-audited slice of every teacher/source/tier; judge disagreements and obvious horror-painting failures are reviewed before data admission.

The first paid decision is after the 120-scene accepted tranche and one mixed SFT, using the frozen held-out comparison. If it improves visual preference without trading away recognizability, expand toward 600 and then run quality RL with the same reference-conditioned pairwise feedback. If it only improves validity or makes images flatter, change the admitted target distribution and the teacher/revision procedure before a longer optimizer run. Introduce a separate speed curriculum only after quality reliably wins. Preserve public hash-verified checkpoints and source/data/config identifiers in the run record; GPU work and rendering run on Linux.
