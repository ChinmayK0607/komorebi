# Blind judging protocol

This benchmark compares teacher-generated painting episodes on the 40 reference images listed in `refs.json`. It is a human visual evaluation plan; this document does not make model calls or admit any data to SFT. The reference gallery is for inspecting the chosen prompt set and its provenance. Judges should see the actual candidate image bytes at a consistent display size, with the reference available in a separate pane, while model, provider, track, checkpoint, turn, and episode identifiers remain hidden.

## Build the offline cross-model packet

After collecting results, build each track's packet from the episode receipts. The command only reads local JSON and image files; it makes no provider or judge calls:

```bash
python3 pairwise_packet.py \
  --root results-ai-gateway-20260923 \
  --reference-root . \
  --track quality \
  --output results-ai-gateway-20260923/review/pairwise/quality
```

Run the same command with `--track screen` or `--track speed` for separate packets. `judge-packet.json` contains only pending eligible comparisons, opaque A/B asset paths, and coverage counts. The exact reference and candidate hashes are checked before copying. `private-mapping.json` records the source model, provider, episode, turn, validity, and failure evidence; keep it with the private review records. No winner, tie, uncertainty, or score is generated by the builder. Pairs with one or two invalid candidates remain in `censored` and in the coverage denominator and do not become visual losses.

## Evaluation matrix

| Track | Episodes | Turns / response cap | Primary pairings | First-to-final pairs |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Screen | 8 references × 5 models = 40 | Native 6-turn cap; 4–6 target, early finish allowed | 8 × C(5, 2) = 80 | 40 |
| Quality | 40 references × 5 models = 200 | Native 12-turn cap | 40 × C(5, 2) = 400 | 200 |
| Speed | 40 references × 5 models = 200 | 3 turns, 8,192 max tokens, 900-second episode timeout | 40 × C(5, 2) = 400 | 200 |
| **All three, run separately** | **440 episodes** |  | **880 pair comparisons** | **440** |

The primary comparisons are the ten unordered model pairs for each reference in each track. A reversed A/B presentation is an optional consistency check; it doubles presentations but does not create additional independent model pairs. First-to-final comparisons are within the same model/reference episode and are reported descriptively, not mixed into the cross-model leaderboard.

Screen, quality and speed results remain separate. A model can be strong under one budget and weak under another; do not pool tracks into one quality claim.

## What the judge sees and records

For each pair, show the reference and two candidate canvases labelled only **A** and **B**. Randomize which candidate is A independently for every pair. Do not show filenames, model names, provider names, checkpoint or turn labels, request timing, token usage, or failure paths in the judging view. Record the private mapping separately from the blind response.

Record one row per attempted comparison:

- reference ID and private episode/pair ID;
- track and hidden candidate IDs in the private mapping;
- whether each candidate is a valid, complete final painting;
- `winner`: `A`, `B`, `tie`, or `uncertain`;
- confidence: `high`, `medium`, or `low`;
- a short fidelity note;
- a short aesthetic note;
- the chosen image's most important weakness (or `none apparent`);
- a concise uncertainty or tie reason when applicable;
- failure class and operational reason when a candidate is not a valid painting.

A tie means the images are acceptably indistinguishable for the stated criteria. Use uncertain when the display, image content, or evidence is insufficient for a reliable choice. Do not force a winner to eliminate ties or uncertainty.

## Holistic pairwise rubric

First check validity. A candidate must have a complete, viewable final canvas associated with the episode's final response. A missing canvas, renderer/service error, malformed output, or explicitly invalid model trace is a failure for accounting purposes, not a low aesthetic score. Do not infer quality from a filename, partial render, or an unrelated stale PNG.

For two valid candidates, choose the image that better satisfies the whole brief. Use these dimensions to structure the judgment rather than assigning an automatic arithmetic score:

1. **Fidelity to the reference.** Compare the main subject, count and placement of important objects, pose and geometry, perspective, color relationships, lighting, and salient negative space. Reward recognizable structure and deliberate correspondence, not pixel-level copying.
2. **Aesthetic finish.** Compare composition, visual hierarchy, shape and edge control, color harmony, material cues, coherence of marks, and whether the painting reads as intentional and complete. Penalize muddiness, accidental seams, broken forms, distracting repetition, and visibly unfinished areas.
3. **Overall preference.** Weigh fidelity and finish together. A beautiful image that changes the reference's subject or composition may lose to a less polished but faithful image; a faithful image with severe rendering defects may lose to a coherent, finished interpretation.

The judge's notes should mention the concrete evidence behind the choice, such as “preserves the two overlapping animals and their scale” or “stronger color grouping but the foreground hand is malformed.” Avoid unsupported claims about the model or training process.

## Failures, denominators, and reporting

Keep two accounting streams:

- **Benchmark success:** for every model and track, report attempts, valid final canvases, model-invalid outputs, renderer failures, service/timeouts, missing assets, and any other censored outcome. Report the valid-final rate over all attempted episodes. Preserve raw failure reasons and evidence paths.
- **Aesthetic comparisons:** include a comparison in the visual win/tie/uncertainty denominator only when both candidates are valid actual images. Exclude one-or-both-failure pairs from aesthetic wins and losses, and report those censored pair counts separately. Never turn a failure into a visual loss or silently drop it.

For valid pairs, publish wins, losses, ties, and uncertain decisions with their denominators for each model, track, and reference where useful. Ties and uncertain decisions are not wins. If reverse-order checks are run, report agreement, disagreement, and unresolved cases; disagreements should widen uncertainty rather than be silently resolved by order.

First-to-final comparisons use the same validity rule and have at most 40 screen pairs or 200 quality/speed pairs. Report how many first and final images were valid before describing improvement or regression. They are a within-episode diagnostic, not evidence that one model beats another.

Do not claim that a win rate establishes general artistic quality. Include the reference-set scope, track budget, validity denominator, tie/uncertainty counts, and any missing or censored episodes beside every headline result. This benchmark alone does not authorize automatic SFT admission; admission requires human review of the raw images and a matched baseline under the same protocol.

## Provenance and reproducibility

Archive the exact `refs.json`, its recorded source-manifest hash, `config.json`, `contract.json`, `model-catalog.json`, prompt, private episode manifests, candidate asset hashes, and judge rows. Keep credentials and provider connection details out of those records. The reference JPGs are linked by the static gallery without byte transformation, and their per-image SHA-256 values are checked against `refs.json` before gallery generation.
